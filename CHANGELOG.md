# Changelog

All notable changes to **Tadah.MLIP** (the MLIP module — high-level training / predict / data-balance orchestration) are documented here.
Format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

> Note: the umbrella project that ships the `tadah` binary is also called **Tadah.MLIP**; its changelog lives at the umbrella repo root and aggregates across all modules. This file is the per-module log for the `MLIP/` library layer.

### Changed
- **BREAKING — the stress metrics measure the stress tensor: its nine entries, in GPa.** `Analytics::calc_s_rmse_gpa` is the root mean square of the nine entries of each residual tensor (the six entries x <= y, an off-diagonal one twice: the Frobenius norm a fit with `STRESS true` minimises, the same for a rotated cell), and `calc_s_mae_gpa` their mean absolute value, both in GPa (1 GPa = 6.241509074e-3 eV/Å³, `kGPaInEvPerA3`); `calc_s_r_sq` reads the nine entries. `analytics_grad::srmse_gpa_per_dataset` gives the partials of each dataset's RMSE with respect to the six predicted entries (an off-diagonal partial for both of its entries), and `srmse_gpa` those of their mean over the measured datasets. `tadah predict -S -A` prints the stress MAE and RMSE in GPa over the nine entries (six entries in eV/Å³ in 1.5.0), and `tadah hpo`'s `PC_SRMSE_GPA` (Tadah.HPO) uses them. Tests: `test_stress_metric_gpa.cpp`, `test_analytics_weight0.cpp`.
- **The starter target of `tadah refit` and `refit --edit` weights the validation RMSEs by 1/a² for the accuracy a of the default error scale of each quantity** (`write_hpotarget`): `PC_ERMSE 0 1` (1 meV/atom, `ESIGMA`), the commented `PC_FRMSE 0 100` (0.1 eV/Å, `FSIGMA`) and `PC_SRMSE_GPA 0 4` (0.5 GPa, `SSIGMA`), so under `LOSS L2` each term counts its RMSE in units of a. In 1.5.0 the commented force line had the weight 1, and there was no stress line. `refit --retarget` writes no such line. Test: `test_stress_metric_gpa.cpp`.
- **BREAKING — a fit minimises the sum over its observations of priority × (residual / error scale)²** (with the `LAMBDA` penalty), from one unit (`row_weights.h`, above) that `DesignMatrix` (both builds), the intercept check and the weight covariance use. The energy of a structure of N atoms is one observation, its residual divided by N (`ENORM ATOM`) or sqrt(N) (`ENORM SQRT`) over `ESIGMA` (meV/atom); each force component one, over sqrt(a² + (b M)²) (`FSIGMA a b`, eV/Å, M the RMS of the structure's own reference forces); the stress tensor nine entries (each off-diagonal one twice, so a rotated cell fits as the cell) over `SSIGMA a b` (GPa, 1 GPa = 6.241509074e-3 eV/Å³). The priority of an observation is the structure's `eweight`, `fweight` or `sweight` times the `WDBFILE` value of its dataset, which the fit applies (the C++ API included; the load transforms no longer multiply the data's priorities by it, so `WDBFILE 0` leaves a dataset out of the fit only). A row that is zero in every column (a force that is zero by symmetry) is not an observation. `ENERGY false` fits forces or stresses only. A fit (M_BLR, M_KRR `train`, `tadah train` before it reads the data) refuses what `require_fit_settings` refuses, and writes an INFO line of its error scales (`Fit error scales: ESIGMA 1 meV/atom (default), ENORM ATOM (default), ENERGY true (default), FSIGMA 0.1 0 eV/Å (default; FORCE false), SSIGMA 0.5 0 GPa (default; STRESS false).`); a potential it writes has `ESIGMA`, `FSIGMA`, `SSIGMA`, `ENORM` and `ENERGY` lines, the defaults included. In 1.5.0 the sum ran over the total energies, the force components and six stress entries, each times the global `EWEIGHT`, `FWEIGHT` or `SWEIGHT` and the structure's weight, so a fit of the same data and settings gives other coefficients, and a `LAMBDA` weighs against another sum. Tests: `test_error_scales.cpp`, `test_row_weights.cpp`, and the references of `test_predict_error.cpp`, `test_nonlinear_model_training.cpp` and `test_descriptors_calc_bias.cpp`, re-derived from the rule.
- **BREAKING — the retired keys (`EWEIGHT`, `FWEIGHT`, `SWEIGHT`, `WDBFILE_AUTO`, `EWEIGHT_TEMP`; Tadah.CORE) in the programs.** Every load of a potential that has them works, with one notice that lists them (`tadah predict`, `tadah properties pairwise`, `tadah analysis`, `tadah refit --edit`); every fit refuses a configuration that has one, with the conversion of each (`tadah train`, `train -c` of a potential or of a file that includes one with `CONFIG`, the C++ API through `M_BLR` and `M_KRR` `train`); `--wdbfile-auto` and `--eweight-temp` give the conversion (`TaskFactory`); `tadah refit --edit` keeps a potential's retired lines as they are and adds no error scale line (`write_potential_with_record` writes the retired lines a parse recorded), so a fit of the edited potential is refused as one of its input; `tadah explain <KEY>` gives a retired key's reason, replacement and conversion. Prediction neither checks nor reads the error scales: a potential trained with `FORCE true` and a non-default `FSIGMA` predicts without `-F`. Tests: `test_retired_keys_fit.cpp` (on a potential that `tadah train` 1.5.0 wrote, `tests_data/potentials_1_5/`), `test_explain_engine.cpp`.
- **`WNOISE` is the noise variance of an observation of priority 1 in the units of the data**, s² σ², σ the absolute error scale of the type (`ESIGMA`/1000 eV per atom, or per sqrt(atom) under `ENORM SQRT`; `FSIGMA` a; `SSIGMA` a in eV/Å³, that of a diagonal entry): `tadah predict --error` adds N^2q WNOISE E to the variance of the energy of a structure of N atoms, WNOISE F to a force component and WNOISE S (half of it off the diagonal) to a stress entry, and refuses a `WNOISE` without an `ESIGMA` line beside it, which a fit before the error scales wrote in other units. Tests: `test_weight_covariance_io.cpp`, `test_predict_error.cpp`.
- The messages of the metrics, of `tadah predict -A`, of the DFT readers and of the fits call the per-structure and dataset multipliers priorities (`no dataset has a structure with an energy (energy priority 0 (eweight) everywhere)`, `stored with zero stress and stress priority 0`), and fitted numbers coefficients.
- **BREAKING — a priority of the training data is a finite number >= 0.** A dataset file whose priorities line (`eweight fweight sweight`) has a negative value is refused as it is read, naming the file, the structure, its label and the value (`DBFILE a.tadah: structure 2: structure 'second': eweight '-1' is negative: a priority is a number >= 0 ...`); every fit of `M_BLR` and `M_KRR` (`M_KRR` implementation 2 included) refuses, before anything else reads the priorities, a structure's priority that is negative or not finite (one given through the C++ API), naming it, the structure (index and label) and its dataset, and a `WDBFILE` value that is not a finite number >= 0 or a `WDBFILE` without one value per dataset, naming the dataset (`require_valid_weights`, `data_weights.h`); with `BIAS true` such a priority is refused as such, not as intercepts the data cannot determine. In 1.5.0 two negative weights of a row cancelled (`EWEIGHT -1` with every `eweight` -1 fitted as if both were 1), one was refused as a value of the design matrix that a descriptor gave, and a negative `WDBFILE` was applied.
- **BREAKING — a structure with energy weight 0 has no energy of its own** (its energy line, which the dataset format needs, holds a placeholder), and everything that reads energies leaves it out, as training does: `ESHIFT_ATOM` (the isolated atoms) and `ESHIFT_DBATOM` (the least-squares fit, whose shift of a species that only such structures contain is 0, with a WARNING naming it, while the other species are fitted), `EFILTER` (it is not dropped for its energy), the reference state of the log, the energy metrics (`Analytics::calc_e_mae`, `calc_e_rmse`, `calc_e_rrmse`, `calc_e_r_sq`) and their partials (`analytics_grad::emae`, `ermse_per_dataset`, `ermse`, `errmse`, `ersq`), the energy statistics of `compute_data_stats` (`tadah analysis dataset_stats`, and the statistics `suggest_bounds` and `check_bounds` read) and the standard deviations the design matrix logs. A dataset whose structures all have energy weight 0 is not measured by the energy metrics (nan, `MetricQuantity::Energy` of `measured_datasets`, `measured_mean` and `require_metric_structures`); `tadah predict -A` leaves it out of the mean and names it in a note. A structure with force weight 0 is left out of `FFILTER` and of the force statistics. In 1.5.0 the placeholder energies and forces were counted (an `ESHIFT_ATOM` shift averaged with a placeholder 0, a dataset's energy RMSE included structures without an energy).
- **BREAKING — M_KRR implementation 2 (`MODEL M_KRR <kernel> 2`) refuses what its solve ignores**: it solves (K + `LAMBDA` I) w = t on the mean descriptors of whole structures and their energies per atom, so `FORCE true`, `STRESS true`, `ENERGY false`, `ENORM SQRT`, an `ESIGMA`, `FSIGMA` or `SSIGMA` other than its default (compared by value), a `WDBFILE` value other than 1 and a structure whose `eweight` is not 1 are refused before the fit, each naming the key and its values (or the structure) and implementation 1, which fits them (`MODEL M_KRR Kern_RBF 2 fits the energies per atom of whole structures, without forces, stresses, error scales or priorities: FORCE true would be ignored. Implementation 1 (MODEL M_KRR Kern_RBF 1) fits the forces; implementation 2 fits with FORCE false.`). 1.5.0 fitted the same model with or without them. The fit of precomputed descriptors (`train(StDescriptorsDB&, StructureDB)`), which runs implementation 1, refuses `MODEL M_KRR <kernel> 2`, naming `train(StructureDB, DescriptorsCalc)`; 1.5.0 fitted implementation 1 under it. `refuse_what_krr_implementation2_ignores(context, stdb)` (`data_weights.h`; its text `krr_implementation2_ignores`) makes these checks on a configuration and its data without a fit, `require_fit_lambda(context)` (`weight_provisioning.h`) checks the `LAMBDA` of the fit of a configuration (that of implementation 2, or of `LinearRegressor::train`) without a fit, `fitted_intercept_columns(context, f)` (`intercepts.h`) gives the intercept columns the fit of a configuration fits (those `FIXINDEX` does not pin), and `require_oalgo3_intercepts(context, f)` refuses, with the fit's message, `OALGO 3` with a `LAMBDA` other than 0 when there is one (Tadah.MODELS `LinearRegressor::require_oalgo3_intercepts`); tadah hpo calls them when a run starts.
- **BREAKING (C++ API) — `RefitEngine::compact_fit` returns nothing, and `tadah refit` has no dense fallback in compact mode** (`refit_engine.h`): the hook fits the table, filling the fit and its self-test, or throws; a hook that returns without a fit is `std::logic_error` in `run()` (`tadah refit: internal error: the compact fitter returned without a fit (RefitEngine::compact_fit fills the fit and its self-test, or throws); this is a bug, please report it`). With only blank and comment lines `tadah --task` read the first element of an empty list and crashed (exit status 139, `SIGSEGV`), and `MLIPUserConfigParser` did the same when it merged the sections of such an input (it now returns none); with only `GLOBAL` keys it ran nothing and exited 0. A `--config` file without keys no longer reads past the empty list either. Test: `test_user_config_sections.cpp`.
- `Scheduler::execute` returns the first non-zero engine status (and stops) instead of discarding it, so a failed task no longer exits with success.
- The test `write_optim_block: scalar -> (1) ; vector -> (1-N)` never ran under CTest: the vendored Catch discovery script split its name at the `;` into two entries, which matched no test case and still passed. It now runs and passes.
- **`DIMER` models: every interatomic distance was zero.** `DescriptorsCalc::calc_dimer` assigned each displacement to the copy that `Mat6R3C::row()` returns, so the six distances of a molecule pair stayed zero and every configuration got the same descriptors, in training and in `tadah predict`. The displacements are written with `Mat6R3C::set_row()`, and `row()` returns a const copy (CORE 1.6.0), so the assignment that lost them no longer compiles. `pair_style tadah` computes its own distances and was not affected; `tadah predict` with the published `Tadah!N2` potential now gives the energies LAMMPS gives. Test: `test_descriptors_calc_dimer.cpp`.
- **Potential files are written losslessly.** `TrainEngine::dumpPotFile` (train, `refit`, `refit --edit`) writes through `Context::write_potential`: every non-integer number with NUMERIC significant digits, default 18, so a potential reloaded from pot.tadah is bit-identical to the one in memory. NUMERIC is never written into the file; an explicit NUMERIC below 17 is honoured with a WARNING, which names the keys written exactly whatever NUMERIC is: the cutoffs and radial grids, `RCUT2B`, `RCUTMB`, `CGRID2B`, `CGRIDMB`, `SGRID2B` and `SGRIDMB` (Tadah.CORE: blueprint field `exact_in_potential`), below 17 as the shortest text that reads back as the same value, because a load checks them exactly (Tadah.MODELS' setfl-cutoff check of D2_EAM and DM_EAM, a knot channel's cutoff, the support of a `Cut_Dummy` bump of `D2_Blip`, `D2_Blip5` or a DM_REAM bump density); every other number follows NUMERIC. `tadah refit` now honours NUMERIC from its configuration (it was ignored). `refit --edit` honours a NUMERIC set for its run, with a WARNING, and no longer promises an identical prediction when NUMERIC < 17; a NUMERIC line of the edited potential (every 1.5.0 `tadah hpo` potential has `NUMERIC 12`) is not read (the BREAKING entry of the training-record keys under *Changed*). Tests: `test_dump_pot_file.cpp` (also `dumpPotFile: NUMERIC below 17 writes the cutoff and grid keys exactly and rounds the other numbers`, with a D2_Knot5 potential whose cutoff is its largest knot), `test_refit_engine.cpp`, `test_refit_edit_mode.cpp`.
- **LSCALE / ESHIFT are stamped into pot.tadah exactly.** They had 6 significant digits, so predict and HPO applied a slightly different transform than training; newly trained potentials may differ from older ones by up to ~1e-5 eV/atom. Test: `test_load_transforms.cpp`.
- Readers accept subnormal values (`core::to_double`): FIXWEIGHT, the energy-only line of a dataset structure, `refit --retarget` bounds, hpotarget contracts, VASP POSCAR coordinates and CASTEP cell numbers. A FIXWEIGHT written as `1e-310` failed with "FIXWEIGHT entry is not a number", and a POSCAR exported by `tadah data write` with a subnormal coordinate failed to load. Tests: `test_weight_provisioning.cpp`, `test_dataset_precision.cpp`.
- **`Structure::save` writes the z force, and every double exactly.** The z force column held `force(1)` (the y component); weights and energy had 6 significant digits, and cell, stress, positions and forces 6 decimals. Every double now has 17 significant digits, so `save()` -> `read()` round-trips bit-exactly; `save(path)` throws when the file cannot be opened or written, the caller's stream format is restored, and a one-letter symbol no longer writes a NUL byte. `Structure::save` has no CLI caller (public API only). Test: `test_structure.cpp`.
- **`--retarget` recovers lower-bound-clamped lines with their own group's percentage.** A line whose lower bound was clamped (the width channels, RCUT scalars, and now non-negative amplitudes) was recovered from its upper bound with the file header's percentage, while a per-key retarget (`--pct 5 SGRIDMB=50`) rewrites only that group's `# KEY  (+-X%` comment; repeating such a retarget moved the recovered centres, so the file was not a fixed point. The group comment's percentage is used now (the header's for groups without one).
- `refit --retarget`: a per-key percentage on an `RCUT2B`/`RCUTMB` scalar line is recorded in a group comment above the line (`# RCUT2B  (+-50%, 1 params)`, removed again at the global percentage), so `RCUT2B=50` run twice is byte-identical; the second run recovered the centre with the file header's percentage. `--pct`/`--set` numbers are read with `core::parse_decimal_normal` (the hpotarget grammar) instead of `core::to_double`, which took `5x`, `0x10`, `inf`, `nan` and subnormals. `--pct KEY=N` for a key without an `OPTIM` line is an error. A `--set` needs finite bounds, `lo < hi`, and a `lo` the density form's rule admits (`optim_low_bound_rule(...)->admits`: NaN is refused). The `RHOFORM` header error names its line. Tests: `test_refit_retarget.cpp` (with the missing fail-before test of the per-group percentage, `SGRID2B=50` run twice).
- `check_bounds_at_corners` requires `RHOFORM` for a DM_REAM seed (`require_rhoform`; `stored_rhoform` let a seed without it pass without the density rules), takes the seed's basis for the rules (`optim_rule_basis`: the Gaussian `SGRIDMB` rule under `NONNEG`), and builds each corner with `corner_context`, which sets only the bound's entry: a vector key was replaced by `arg_pos + 1` copies of the corner value (`(1)` by one), and two bounds on one key overwrote each other. Tests: `test_check_bounds.cpp`.
- `check_bounds_at_corners` audits every corner against `models::optim_low_bound_rule(key, seed)`, the rule `tadah hpo` refuses a box by: besides the density-form rules, every radial bump width > 0 in every form (`SGRIDMB` of a DM_REAM `Blip`/`Blip5` density, `SGRID2B` of a `D2_Blip`/`D2_Blip5` pair term), so a width box reaching 0 or below fails its low corners (a `NONNEG` `Blip5` `SGRIDMB` box from -0.5 passed). A finding names what sets the rule (`RHOFORM NONNEG requires ...`, `the D2_Blip5 pair basis requires every SGRID2B width > 0 ...`). Tests: `test_check_bounds.cpp`.
- `TrainEngine::dumpPotFile` and `write_hpotarget` throw, naming the path, when the output cannot be opened or written; they logged an ERROR / a WARNING and returned, so `tadah train -o no_such_dir/pot.tadah` exited 0. Test: `test_dump_pot_file.cpp`.
- `tadah analysis bfunc` applies `check_embf0_precedence` and `check_rhoform_precedence` to the potential and the task before merging them, as predict and analysis descriptor do. Test: `test_rhoform_pot_key.cpp`.
- **The CIF reader (and the COD reader, which uses it) stores lattice vector aᵢ as cell row i.** It copied gemmi's orthogonalisation matrix, whose columns are the lattice vectors, untransposed: every non-orthogonal CIF/COD structure (hexagonal, trigonal, monoclinic, triclinic) had a transposed cell while its atoms were placed correctly (hcp Mg got a 2.78 Å neighbour instead of 3.20 Å). `tadah predict -s`, `tadah data print|write -s` and the analysis tasks used that cell, and `data write` exported it to VASP, LAMMPS and CASTEP files. Test: `test_structure_readers_cell.cpp`.
- **The AFLOW reader places the atoms from `positions_fractional`.** It built the cell from `geometry` (a along x, b in the xy plane) but took the atoms from `positions_cartesian`, which AFLOW gives in its own frame, so a multi-atom entry did not fit its cell (rock-salt NaCl: a 1.91 Å Na–Cl distance instead of 2.82 Å). An entry without `geometry` (it became a 1 Å cube) or without `positions_fractional` is refused, and so are an entry whose species, composition counts and positions do not match (the documented fallback labelled the atoms `X`, which is no element, so it threw "Unknown symbol: X"; two species with one count read past the counts, and one species with two counts dropped a site), a site without exactly three numbers (a missing one became 0), and angles no cell has (they were clamped to a flat cell). Test: `test_structure_readers_cell.cpp`.
- **POSCAR reader: VASP's scaling rules.** Cartesian coordinates are multiplied by the scaling factor, as the lattice is (they were read unscaled, so a Cartesian POSCAR with a factor other than 1 put the atoms in the wrong place); a negative factor is the cell volume (it inverted and inflated the cell); three factors scale the x, y and z components of the lattice vectors (and of Cartesian coordinates); a zero factor, two factors, a non-positive one of three, or no number is refused. The coordinate mode and the Selective dynamics line are recognised by their first character as VASP does (`C`/`K` for Cartesian, `S` for Selective dynamics; `K`, `Kartesian` and `Sel dyn` were missed, and `Direct coordinates (not Cartesian)` was read as Cartesian). A file without the element-symbol line (the VASP 4 layout) is refused with a message saying so (it failed with "Unknown symbol: X"), and so is a symbol line and count line of different lengths (it read past the symbols). Test: `test_structure_readers_cell.cpp`.
- **CASTEP `.cell` reader: units and block order.** The optional unit line of `LATTICE_CART`, `LATTICE_ABC` and `POSITIONS_ABS` is applied (`ang`, `bohr`/`a0`, `nm`, `cm`, `m`, `pm`, any case; it was skipped, so a cell in bohr was read 1.89× too small); `POSITIONS_FRAC` is converted after the whole file is read, so the blocks may come in any order (positions before the lattice all landed at the origin). Text after `!`, `#` or `;` is a comment on any line, as in CASTEP (a commented unit line was taken for data, and a header such as `%BLOCK POSITIONS_FRAC!sites` dropped its atoms). A file without a lattice block or a positions block (it gave a structure with no atoms), a `LATTICE_CART` without exactly three vectors (a fourth wrote past the cell), an incomplete `LATTICE_ABC`, a singular lattice, an unknown unit and a positions block without atoms are refused. Test: `test_dataset_readers_cell.cpp`.
- **Data migration: datasets converted by an older Tadah may hold a wrong cell.** Row i of the cell block is lattice vector aᵢ (`Structure::cell`, *Dataset Format*). Only structures with a non-symmetric cell can be affected; `tadah analysis dataset_stats` lists them. Re-convert them from their source files if they were converted: (1) before 1.3.0-beta.1 (MLIP `c6d457c`, 2026-05-07) from a VASP OUTCAR or a CASTEP `.castep`, `.md` or `.geom` file — the cell was stored transposed (the positions are right: transposing the 3×3 cell block repairs it); (2) before 1.3.0-beta.1 from a vasprun.xml — the cell is right, the positions are not (r_new = cellᵀ·cell⁻¹·r_old repairs them). Structure files that `tadah data write -s` exported (VASP, LAMMPS, CASTEP) from a non-orthogonal CIF or COD entry before 1.6.0 hold a transposed cell (see above): export them again. Potentials and `tadah hpo` results fitted to affected structures need to be regenerated. The 1.3.0-beta.1 change was not recorded in this changelog.
- **`NNFinder` finds every periodic image of any cell.** Its image bound came from the lattice translations shorter than the cutoff instead of the cell heights, so a cell thinner than the cutoff in some direction lost images (a 2-atom bcc cell with a = 3.41 Å at a 10 Å cutoff listed 168 of each atom's 180 neighbours, and the neighbour vectors no longer summed to zero: a perfect crystal got forces); atoms outside the cell and cells whose matrix is not symmetric were binned with wrong fractional coordinates (a sheared cell lost 6 of 80 images, an unwrapped cube 28 of 38 pairs); a tilted cell with every edge above the cutoff but a height below it took the path meant for thick cells; and a tolerance merge of close images made thin-cell tables asymmetric. One search now serves every cell: the lattice basis is reduced (an integer unimodular change of basis, so a skewed description of a benign lattice costs nothing), the atoms go into a cell list over their wrapped fractional coordinates sized by the cell heights, and each pair is tested once with the distance computed as consumers compute it, keeping pairs below cutoff + `NNTable::guard` so every pair a consumer computes inside the cutoff is listed. Shifts are integer triples relative to the stored positions (never changed), each atom's entries are sorted by (j, shift), and the table is symmetric. Thick cells with wrapped atoms get the same pairs within the cutoff in the same order as before (bit-identical results), plus any pair within `NNTable::guard` above the cutoff (a lattice shell exactly at it), which consumers ignore; other structures change — retrain potentials fitted to them. `tadah predict` now agrees with `pair_style tadah`. Tests: `test_nn_finder_exact.cpp` (against the brute-force oracle `tests/nn_oracle.h`), `test_nn_replication_invariance.cpp`.
- **A neighbour-cache hit after a cell change returned the image displacements of the old cell** (`nn_cache::is_fresh` handed back the cached table unchanged): the native relaxer's Iso forces were off by 0.04–0.49 eV/Å and HPO's cohesive-energy observable's E_coh by 0.06–0.08 eV/atom in the tests' setups. A hit with a changed cell now gets a copy of the table with the displacements of the current cell (the same table while the cell is bitwise unchanged). Tests: `test_nn_cache.cpp`, `test_native_relaxer.cpp`.
- **`compute_data_stats` (`tadah analysis dataset_stats`, the pre-flight audit)** rebuilds a structure's neighbour list when it was built with a smaller cutoff than the audit's (it counted from the short list), reports a structure NNFinder refuses as an exception naming it (it called `std::terminate` from its OpenMP loop), and counts neighbours strictly inside its cutoff, as the model does, so the counts stay the same now that a list may hold pairs within `NNTable::guard` above its cutoff (simple cubic a = 2.5 at 5 Å: 26 neighbours, not the 32 of the list). Test: `test_data_stats_compute.cpp`.
- `NNFinder::calc(StructureDB)` called `std::terminate` when a structure's search threw inside its OpenMP loop (the image cap on a degenerate cell); it now rethrows the error naming the structure.
- **`tadah analysis bfunc`**: a knot basis with mixed degrees (`KDEG2B`, `KDEGMB`) is analysed with its degrees and without widths, which knot potentials do not have (it read a width array); `--rescale --derivative` prints the product rule f_c g' + f_c' g (it printed f_c g'); a DM_REAM density is analysed in its declared form (the steps of a `MONOTONE` density) and its embedding with its reference. Tests: `Knot basis analysis accepts absent scales mixed degrees and cutoff product rule`, `Basis analysis of a DM_REAM density keeps the meaning of Y and --rescale`.
- **A refit of an EAM file refuses the options of `--retarget` and `--edit`** set in its configuration (`RETARGET_RCUT2B`, `RETARGET_RCUTMB`, `PCT`, `SET`, `RETARGET_RHOFORM`, `FUNC`, `ADD`, `REMOVE`, `ETA`, `DEG`: `tadah refit: the RETARGET_RCUT2B key requires --retarget <file.hpotarget>.`), as each mode refuses the other's: the plain refit did not look at them, so they had no effect (also in 1.5.0). ## [1.4.0] — 2026-07-14

### Added
- **`--retarget --rcut2b <r> / --rcutmb <r>`**: declare a bump channel's fixed cutoff in the same run — the file's `OPTIM RCUT*` reference line is recentred on `r`, stray ACTIVE `OPTIM RCUT*` lines are commented out with a warning (searching RCUT on a gated bump channel is a wasted dimension), and the rewritten CGRID/SGRID boxes are truncated to `c + w/eta <= r`. Replaces the two-step `--set 'RCUT2B(1)=lo,hi'` + `--pct` dance after manually raising a pot's cutoff.

### Changed
- **hpotarget writer: bump channels under a hard (Cut_Dummy) cutoff get gate-compliant OPTIM boxes.** Per blip, the emitted CGRID upper / SGRID lower bounds are capped (directionally quantized, so the cap is exact on the bound grid) to the bump-support invariant `c_hi + w/eta_lo <= RCUT` that `tadah hpo` now hard-errors on, and a parsed contract comment (`# RCUT2B bump-support contract: w = ...`) records the half-width for `--retarget`. A pot whose own bump spills is emitted uncapped with a warning (the repair tool `refit --edit remove` also writes an hpotarget, so the writer never throws); knot/Gaussian/FIXWEIGHT emissions are byte-identical to before.
- **`--retarget` understands the bump-support contract**: recovers the centre of a hi-capped CGRID line from its LOWER bound (mirror of the existing lo-clamped SGRID repair), re-applies the cap after every rewrite (fresh(pct) -> retarget(pct) stays byte-identical; any retargeted file is byte-stable), and lets `--set` win with a warning when it violates the contract corner.
- `--retarget`: with duplicate or ACTIVE `OPTIM RCUT*` lines the bump-support cap now binds against the smallest reachable cutoff (active line -> its rewritten LOW bound, commented -> centre) and warns, instead of trusting whichever line comes first in the file.
- `refit --edit --add` passes the pot's cutoff kind to `potedit::add_basis`: a bump support crossing rcut is a hard error for Cut_Dummy pots (with the minimal `--eta` in the message), a warning for smooth cutoffs.

## [1.3.0-beta.1] — 2026-05-20

The MLIP layer gains in-place HPO support, load-time dataset transforms, and a wave of correctness fixes.
